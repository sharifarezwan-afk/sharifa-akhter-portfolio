About

Every number should trace back to its source.

Business intelligence taught me to ask first whether the business can trust the number.

I am a Data Analyst and BI Developer with 5+ years of experience in SQL, PostgreSQL, MS SQL Server and Power BI, building reporting and dashboards across mortgage lending, banking, oil and gas, and medical devices. Most of that work is about data quality and reporting accuracy: modeling the data properly, documenting the architecture, and explaining the result to people who will never write a DAX measure.

I am now applying that habit to AI. I evaluate LLM responses for incorrect reasoning and factual consistency, write and test prompts, label data against clear guidelines, and design human-in-the-loop workflows. Alongside that, I build practical agent projects in Python with LLM APIs: retrieval over documents, SQL-based assistants and automation pipelines.

I am interested in AI training projects involving response evaluation, prompt creation, structured reasoning, domain-specific annotation and quality assurance for AI systems.

-- A typical time-intelligence measure behind a yearly / quarterly view
Funded Volume YoY % =
VAR CurrentPeriod = [Funded Volume]
VAR PriorPeriod =
    CALCULATE ( [Funded Volume], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR ( 'Date'[Date] ) )
RETURN
    DIVIDE ( CurrentPeriod - PriorPeriod, PriorPeriod )

Measures, not magic

Patterns like this sit behind the yearly, quarterly, monthly and daily reports I build. The same instinct carries into AI evaluation: define the comparison clearly, handle the edge cases, and make the result reproducible.
